# Design philosophy

*These are design goals, not conclusions about solved balance.*

### Territory should create decisions

An Outpost opens construction reach and adds defense. Losing it can undermine an economic plan. The value of an expansion depends on whether it can be protected.

### Information should be worth investing in

Recon can generate both knowledge and rewards, but sending more infantry into the field sacrifices other opportunities and exposes them to danger. Neither Mine-first nor Recon-first should become the only interesting opening.

### A powerful unit can still create strategic variety

The Scout Car dominates much of the current Base 1 military environment. That does not imply the game has a single strategy: timing, cost, limits, positioning, Recon hunting, defensive duties and the need to hold captured ground can distinguish two players using the same unit class. Later technologies should broaden the answers to it.

### The condition of the army matters

Stamina, squad leadership, Sergeant traits, positioning, morale and injuries are meant to change battle outcomes. The aim is not deterministic hero units, but a battlefield where preparation and tactical judgment can sometimes overcome raw numbers.

### Preservation has a cost and a reward

A fight does not end when the last shot is fired. Recovering soldiers, protecting vehicles, reinforcing an Outpost and deciding when to retreat should be meaningful strategic acts.

### Progression should change the questions

Advanced vehicles and specialists should add new military relationships. Heavier threats to buildings should make siege a meaningful later-game choice; Base 1 capture and mobility should remain distinct from those later battles.

### Players should understand what happened

Interesting hidden calculations are not enough. Gameplay feedback, low-poly art readability, HUD design, animation and sound must make important conditions visible.
