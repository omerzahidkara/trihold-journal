# 04 — When Six Soldiers Lose to Three

**Focus:** stamina, combat readiness, Sergeant traits and a real test observation.

## The situation

Six attacking infantry are tired after running. Their opponents are a defending group of three: a Sergeant with an **Advanced Guns** trait and two infantry.

In some manual development tests, **the three defenders won**. It did not happen every time.

## Why that is interesting

In the current design, fatigue can reduce infantry shooting effectiveness. Sergeant leadership and traits can alter a squad's capabilities, while positioning, distance, movement and shot outcomes matter as well. This makes the raw headcount only part of the story.

A larger army that is poorly prepared or committed at the wrong moment can sometimes lose to a smaller force.

## What this does not prove

The observation does **not** prove that fatigue alone caused the result, that Advanced Guns alone caused it, or that the outcome is statistically common or competitively balanced.

A responsible test would vary fatigue, range, stance, Sergeant traits and positioning independently, then run enough trials to estimate how often each outcome occurs.

## Player experience question

Can a player understand *why* six soldiers lost? A system becomes frustrating when the answer exists only in hidden mechanics. That makes stamina feedback, recognizable squad leadership, combat readability and interface design essential—not decorative extras.

**Observed:** a real category of developer test outcome. **Still open:** causality, frequency, fairness and readability.
