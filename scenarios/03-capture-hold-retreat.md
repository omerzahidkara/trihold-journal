# 03 — Capture, Hold, or Retreat?

**Focus:** transport, Outpost defenses, infantry capture and counterattacks.

## The situation

A Scout Car approaches an enemy Outpost with a formed Sergeant-led squad aboard. It can deliver the infantry toward a less-protected part of the position, but the Outpost's machine gun threatens the vehicle. Leaving it within firing range for too long may result in a major loss.

If the attackers eliminate the protected defenders, the infantry may attempt capture. The existing capture core requires **one Sergeant and at least two assigned, healthy Recruits** positioned correctly; active combat, a contested position and defenders can interrupt progress.

## The real decision

**Commit to capture.** Risk the infantry and the time needed to take the structure, potentially preserving its territorial value.

**Secure and hold.** Withdraw the vehicle from defensive fire, reinforce the position and prepare for a counterattack. Gaining an Outpost and keeping it are different objectives.

**Retreat.** Preserve the car and surviving squad if the attack is becoming too expensive.

## Why not simply destroy the Outpost?

At Base 1, that is generally not a realistic cost-effective choice. The developer estimates that destroying a well-defended Outpost may require a very large commitment—potentially several Scout Cars, with substantial losses or repeated replacements. This is a qualitative design assessment, not a formal performance benchmark. Later IFV and tank-era capabilities are intended to make attacking structures a much more practical option.

## Counterplay

Even if the capture succeeds, the attacker may lose the transport. Enemy reinforcements or a newly produced Scout Car could then eliminate the stranded infantry and retake the position. The associated Mine economy can also be affected by the change in territorial support.

## Evidence boundary

**Implemented:** Scout Car troop transport, Outpost garrison and capture behavior, with scoped manual testing.

**Not guaranteed:** this exact raid succeeding in a live match, or a fixed outcome for a counterattack.

**Design question:** When is taking ground worth the risk of being unable to hold it?
