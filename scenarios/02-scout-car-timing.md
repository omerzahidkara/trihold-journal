# 02 — The Scout Car Timing Window

**Focus:** military timing, vehicle limits, Recon hunting and the economic cost of mobilization.

## The situation

Two players have expanded into the map. Each knows Scout Cars are unusually important in Base 1. Infantry have a very hard time destroying a car, while Outposts offer stronger firepower but may be captured by an infantry squad.

Scout Cars are expensive, and there is a limit to how many can be fielded. Building one early may give a player the first serious mobile threat in the match—but it delays some other investment.

## The decision

One player sends the car hunting enemy Recon. Another holds it near an exposed Outpost. A third possible plan is to embark a squad and search for an opening behind the opponent's defensive line.

All are credible uses of the same vehicle. None can happen at every location simultaneously.

## What makes mirror armies different?

How quickly a player reaches the first Scout Car, how many cars survive, how they use Outpost fire, whether they can catch isolated Recon infantry, and how well they coordinate soldiers and vehicles may matter more than the unit choice alone.

A lost vehicle can be an economic disaster even when the attack initially looked successful.

## Design boundary

The present Base 1 stage is **Scout Car-centered by design and current balance**. That should not be generalized into a claim that Scout Cars will dominate the full game. Base 2 is intended to bring stronger anti-vehicle answers and additional military roles.

**Unproven:** optimal Scout Car build timing or the best car count.
